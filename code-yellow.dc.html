The situation

Cedar's biggest client threatened to churn, triggering a performance bakeoff against Epic, our biggest competitor. I led the Design response, responsible for defining and executing on a comms personalization strategy. 

The bakeoff was measured two ways: First, the client paused Cedar at one location and ran a before/after comparison there. Before they paused us, we had a month to rapidly launch experiments and boost our performance baseline going in. Second, they compared that paused location against a sister location that kept Cedar on — the more direct head-to-head against Epic, where I focused most of the experimentation.

Identifying the opportunity

Data Science funnel and cohort analysis pointed to an opportunity to improve communications for insured patients with big bills.

Tying the opportunity to patient research

After we identified an opportunity to improve top of funnel conversion for patients with big bills, I wanted to dig into who these patients actually are. I connected it to prior UXR research on ALICE patients: Asset-Limited, Income-Constrained, Employed. These patients make too much to qualify for financial assistance, but are still struggling to pay their medical bills in addition to other rising life expenses. 

Understanding these patients shaped how I approached communicating with these patients: Rather than transaction ("here's what you owe"), comms needed to be supportive and directive in helping patients find affordable solutions for managing their rising healthcare costs. 

Subset of ALICE are patients who "treatment drained." They experience a dramatic rise in medical costs that become hard to manage with other life expenses.

Brainstorming comms for ALICE patients

I ran ALICE x Comms brainstorm sessions with the cross-functional project team and the Design team, working from the question: how might we personalize these communications so patients feel more supported in managing and paying their bills? That produced a handful of directions that I then partnered with UXR to concept test with ALICE-lookalike patients. 

Comms concepts that we tested with look alike ALICE patients.

Turning concepts into minimal viable tests

We had a goal of impacting patients 1x a week. This was made possible with an internal email tool I previously designed called Promoter, which allowed us to send one-off emails to targeted lists within a few days. While these emails were much more simple than our typical emails, it allowed us to launch experiments to get a quick signal on our ideas before further eng investment. We called these email experiments minimal viable tests. Here are two examples of them.

Hypothesis: Patients avoid paying because they aren't sure which options will work with their budget. If we recommend the options we think will work best, we expect to see more paying. 

Turning research insight into minimal viable test
Research participants responded well to learning about flexible payment options, even before their bill was ready. We couldn't communicate with patients before their bill was ready due to integration limitations, so I tested this idea by sending real patients payment recommendations early in their billing journey.

EXPERIMENT 02

Converting partial payers into payment plan users

Hypothesis: Patients pay part of their balance and dropoff because they can't afford the rest. If we promote payment plans, we can expect more patients paying off their full balance over time.

Started with a minimal viable test
This idea actually came from the client. To establish credibility fast, we launched a simple one-off email to patients with outstanding balances who'd made a partial payment in the last month educating them on payment plans.  After 30 days, results weren't stat sig but showed positive signal.

Scaling + other moments to recommend payment plans
I saw an opportunity to recommend payment plans to patients earlier in their journey, while they were making the partial payment. After getting client buy-in for this idea, I worked with engineering to add a nudge in our partial payment checkout flow to recommend payment plans to patients. This nudge is dynamic and tries to match patients with a plan based on the amount entered.
We also scaled the one-off email into a proper email that aligned with patient's existing comms cadence. 
Final resultsThe final version of the nudge held up: +18.8% payment plan conversion and +4.2% overall payment conversion, both stat sig. We rolled it out as a general enhancement for all clients.

Team enablement with AI

This project established a repeatable experimentation process that was later adopted into other personalization-focused initiatives. To enable the broader Product team, I created these resources:

  • A designer's manual for comms personalization
  • Claude skills for writing product emails and text messages so non-designers could create comms that aligned with our copy principles and guidelines.
  • Legal x Data documentation, collaborating with Legal team to outlines dos/don't around showing patient data in comms and other unauthenticated states. This later enabled a Legal Agent that enables makers to get product changes reviewed over Slack in minutes as opposed to the previous 1-week turnaround.
